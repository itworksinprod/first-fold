OpenAI rewrites its safety rules as Astra tests the old ones

The lab paused some frontier training while it revisits a framework written before models approached its highest cyber threshold.

3 min read · Checked 5:42 AM ET

What happened

OpenAI said preliminary evaluations of its upcoming Astra model were strong enough that it could not rule out the highest cyber-capability tier in its Preparedness Framework. On August 18, Axios reported that the company was rewriting that framework, had paused two weeks of deployment-focused reinforcement learning, and was keeping its largest planned frontier training run on hold.

Why it matters

This is a rare example of an internal safety threshold changing day-to-day model development. It also exposes a governance problem: capability tests are moving faster than the rules meant to govern them. Astra’s assessed level remains preliminary and comes from the company’s own evaluations, so the pause is more certain than the capability claim.

What to watch

Look for the revised framework, independent evaluations, release restrictions, and a clear account of what controls must be in place before large training runs resume.

The short version

A lab’s safety rulebook is now shaping the training schedule, not just the release memo.

Why it made the paper

A same-day operational change with direct implications for frontier-model development and access.

First Fold Work & Tools · Page 3

New development · High confidence

Microsoft folds Deep Research into a paid workflow

Deep Research is leaving the consumer Copilot app, while Microsoft 365 Premium subscribers keep an in-depth research path through Researcher.

2 min read · Checked 5:44 AM ET

What happened

Microsoft began retiring Deep Research in the consumer Copilot app on August 18. Premium subscribers can continue producing detailed reports with Researcher. Saved work is not being deleted: Premium users can find it through Researcher, while Personal and Family subscribers retain access through chat history. Reports can also be opened and saved in Word.

Why it matters

AI work features are still fluid, and a tool’s name, location, or subscription tier can change while teams are building habits around it. The practical risk is less about lost files today and more about undocumented workflows that depend on a product surface the vendor can consolidate.

What to do

Update internal guides that point to Deep Research. Export important reports to Word, record the prompts and review steps behind recurring research, and confirm which users actually have Premium access before standardizing on Researcher.

The short version

Archive the output—and document the workflow—before the interface changes again.

Why it made the paper

A concrete product change with an immediate action for people already using AI for research at work.

First Fold Security & Privacy · Page 4

Material update · High confidence

France’s tax breach reaches 678,000 people and businesses

Officials began notifying victims as scrutiny turned to why the theft was not detected when the attacker’s access was blocked.

3 min read · Checked 5:47 AM ET

What happened

France’s tax authority, DGFiP, confirmed that a breach affected data tied to 678,000 individuals and businesses. The attacker’s access was blocked at the end of June, but the theft was not identified then. Its scale became clear after a public claim in August. The government convened a crisis meeting, ordered an audit overseen by the national cybersecurity agency, and began victim notifications.

Why it matters

Tax and identity details can make fraud attempts unusually convincing. The incident also highlights a familiar defensive gap: stopping access does not prove that defenders know what was viewed or removed. A later detection can leave victims exposed before notifications begin.

What to do

Reach tax services through a known official address rather than inbound links. Treat tax-themed calls, payment demands, and document requests with suspicion. Security teams should review privileged access, exfiltration telemetry, third-party pathways, and whether notification plans can operate before every forensic question is settled.

Defender’s note

Access blocked is not the same as exfiltration understood. Audit logs and outbound data signals need their own review.

Why it made the paper

A confirmed victim count, an active government response, and immediate fraud implications.
