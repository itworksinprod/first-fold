## Predeclared success criteria

Append exactly one clearly labeled **First Fold editorial question** to What to
watch. Preserve the accepted headline, every earlier unit and order byte-for-byte.
At most 36 question words plus the four-word label; assembled body remains
110–225 words, excluding the headline. Abstention or any failed gate holds the
experiment without a fallback draft.

The question must identify concrete observable evidence worth watching about
the documented relationship between requirements and output quality. It must
not reframe a demonstrated result as unknown, merely repeat the old watch text,
or invent a roadmap, scheduled experiment, deployment, guarantee, product or
absence of evidence. The recorded experiments and potential additional quality
goals must remain distinct. A question has factual presuppositions and cannot
evade checking by ending in a question mark.

The only allowed factual context for the addition is saved MIT passages S1P5
(reported experiments/comparison) and S1P20 (possible additional quality goals).
No future roadmap exists in this captured excerpt. The accepted draft is reading
context, not authority for new assertions. Independent review must judge whether
the question actually adds useful information rather than generic caution.
